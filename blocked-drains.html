Signs your drains need attention

A blocked drain rarely announces itself all at once — usually there are a few warning signs first:

  • Water draining slowly from sinks, showers, or baths
  • Gurgling noises from drains or toilets
  • A persistent bad smell from a drain
  • Water pooling in the yard near an outdoor drain
  • Toilet not flushing properly, or water rising when it does

Catching it early usually means a simpler, cheaper fix — so if something feels off, it's worth getting it looked at before it turns into a Saturday-night emergency.

How we actually find the problem

Rather than guessing, we run a CCTV camera down the drain to see exactly what's going on — roots, a collapsed pipe, grease build-up, whatever it is. Then we clear it with high-pressure jetting, properly, not a quick fix that's back in a month.

What usually causes a blockage

Tree roots

Roots find their way into older clay pipes through tiny cracks and joints over time.

Grease and fat build-up

Cooking fat solidifies inside pipes and gradually narrows the flow until it stops entirely.

Wipes and foreign objects

Even "flushable" wipes don't break down the way toilet paper does — a common cause of blockages.

Ageing or collapsed pipes

Older pipes can shift, crack, or collapse over time, especially with ground movement.
